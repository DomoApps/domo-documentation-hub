## Epic description

### Overview

Domo's new left navigation experience offers a more efficient and user-friendly way to navigate the platform. However, current adoption sits at approximately 20% of users. Goals. We will take a 4-phase approach:

- GA with opt-in (we entered this phase when Left Nav released GA). Left Nav is defaulted on for newly created instances and is available to all customers. Administrators can choose to migrate to Left Nav.
- Increase Admin awareness
- Increase users awareness (DOMO-482750)
- Force all instances to Left Nav

This epic covers Phase 2 only

### Solution

- A banner will appear in the product, visible to Admin users only.
- Banner will have links to KB and to settings where they can choose to enable Left Nav.
- Chameleon tour to introduce users to new nav; to be deployed to all users when they first see Left Nav. Requires the chameleon feature switch to be enabled — the tour banner will not show without it.
- No new feature switches

### Success Metrics

- Measurable increase in the percentage of users actively using the new left nav
- Reduction in user-reported friction or support issues related to navigation
- Admin tooling in place to support org-wide navigation configuration and rollout

# Problem & Opportunity

## Defining the Problem to be Solved
Domo's new navigation experience (Left Nav) has been available for Domo Administrators to opt into since its GA release approximately nine months ago. Adoption has remained significantly below target for two reasons:

- Low awareness — many administrators have never encountered the opt-in because the new navigation is not prominently surfaced in-product

- Functionality gaps — at initial GA, the new navigation was missing capabilities that some administrators depend on, giving them valid reasons to hold off

Both barriers are now being addressed in parallel. Nav Configs is GA and will receive a sub-nav support enhancement in May 2026; Workspaces is going GA in May 2026 with a follow-on enhancement to use Workspaces as navigational elements. As those gaps close, Phase 2 focuses on ensuring administrators know the new experience is ready and understand how to adopt it.
The problem is experienced most acutely by Domo Administrators, who are responsible for configuring their organization's Domo environment and who are the decision-makers for enabling the new navigation. Without a clear in-product signal and structured migration guidance, most administrators have either never encountered the opt-in or have been waiting for the product to be ready.

## Strategic Fit
The Left Nav adoption initiative sits at the intersection of product modernization and operational efficiency. Migrating all customers to the new navigation by November 2026 is a prerequisite for removing the legacy navigation codebase in Q1 2027 — eliminating meaningful tech debt and reducing maintenance overhead for the platform team.
Phase 2 directly supports this goal by driving awareness and informed adoption ahead of the eventual mandatory cut-over (Phase 4). Building adoption voluntarily and early reduces the risk of disruption at cut-over and increases the likelihood that customers arrive at Phase 4 already comfortable with the new experience.
The primary Domo stakeholders are the Navigation platform squad, product leadership tracking the migration timeline, and the customer success organization that will field support if the cut-over is disruptive.

### What customer use cases illustrate the need?
Domo's customer base includes environments with tens of thousands of end users, as well as customers who provide Domo as a service to their own customers (OEM/reseller scenarios). In these environments, a Domo Administrator enabling the new navigation is not a personal setting change — it is an organizational change management decision affecting their entire user base or their customers' user bases.
For these administrators, adopting new nav requires understanding what is changing, communicating it clearly to their users (or to their customers), and managing the transition without disruption. Phase 2 directly addresses this need by ensuring administrators have the information and artifacts they need to make a confident migration decision and execute it painlessly.

## Goals & Outcomes

- Drive adoption to 40% — 40% of Domo instances have new nav enabled instance-wide

- Increase admin awareness — Administrators are informed that the new navigation experience exists, is ready, and can be enabled today

- Reduce migration friction — Administrators have the guidance, KB resources, and in-product tools to migrate their instance confidently and with minimal disruption to their users

How will we measure and report on success?

- Primary metric: % of Domo instances with new nav enabled instance-wide (target: 40%)

- Supporting metrics: banner click-through rate, KB page views, guided tour completion rate

Definition of success: 40% of Domo instances have new nav enabled instance-wide following the Phase 2 rollout.
Leading indicators: Banner impressions, KB article views, guided tour starts
Lagging indicators: % of instances with new nav enabled instance-wide

## Un-Goals
As a result of this, what are the metrics that might move but aren't our primary concern?
Might move in a positive direction:

- End user satisfaction with Domo navigation (a downstream benefit of migration, not a Phase 2 metric)

- Support ticket volume related to navigation confusion (may decrease as users are guided)

- Admin engagement with the Domo admin experience broadly

Might move in a negative direction:

- Short-term support ticket volume may increase as administrators begin migration and encounter questions

- Some administrators may experience temporary disruption as their users adjust to the new navigation

Note: The percentage of instances with new nav enabled must not decrease — any regression should be treated as a blocking incident.

## Not Doing
Features discussed that are out of scope for Phase 2 and may be addressed in a later phase:

- Admin-to-user communication tools (Phase 3) — tools that enable administrators to proactively notify or message their end users about the upcoming navigation change

- End-user trial mode (Phase 3) — the ability for end users to try the new navigation experience before their administrator commits to enabling it instance-wide

- Mandatory cut-over (Phase 4) — forcing all remaining instances onto the new navigation; Phase 2 is purely voluntary/opt-in

- Automated migration tooling — scripted or bulk-enable tools for migrating large customer fleets (not in scope for any current phase)

## Assumptions

- Filling the remaining functionality gaps is sufficient to remove the primary product-readiness barrier for administrators who have been holding off

- Increased awareness alone, combined with a ready product, will be sufficient to drive adoption to 40%

- Administrators are the correct target audience for Phase 2 awareness — end users do not need to be reached until they first encounter the new experience (at which point the guided tour handles orientation)
